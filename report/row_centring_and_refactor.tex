\section{Scope of this note}

This note documents a specific piece of work done on the repository: an audit
of whether row-centring was implemented anywhere in the codebase, why it is
required, and a subsequent reorganisation of the single-file pipeline into
separate modules. It is not the assignment report; it is a record of one
engineering change for the team.

\section{What was found}

Before this change, the only pipeline implemented in the repository
(\texttt{src/ordinal\_pipeline.py}) built a 60-node item--item network from
\emph{raw, uncentred} Spearman correlations computed on a matrix whose missing
cells (structural blanks and ``No Comments'') had been recovered by per-item
regularised proportional-odds ordinal logistic regression. No row-centring
step existed anywhere in the codebase or in the accompanying notebook.

This matters because the grand mean of the raw responses is $+1.14$ on a
$[-2, +2]$ scale: respondents agree with nearly everything (acquiescence
bias). A correlation computed on raw scores is therefore dominated by shared
agreeableness rather than by which specific items a respondent leans toward
or away from.

\section{Why row-centring is required}

For respondent $i$ and item $j$, let $x_{i,j}$ denote the (imputed) encoded
response on the $\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}$ scale. Row-centring replaces $x_{i,j}$ with

\begin{equation}
x'_{i,j} \;=\; x_{i,j} \;-\; \bar{x}_{i\cdot}, \qquad
\bar{x}_{i\cdot} = \frac{1}{60}\sum_{k=1}^{60} x_{i,k}.
\end{equation}

Subtracting each respondent's own mean across all 60 items removes their
personal baseline level of agreement before any correlation is computed.
Spearman correlation does not do this automatically: it is invariant to a
monotonic transform applied to a single column, not to an additive shift that
differs row by row. Centring is applied strictly \emph{after} missing-value
imputation (imputation still predicts on the original $-2$ to $+2$ scale) and
strictly \emph{before} the correlation step, since it is a network-construction
transform, not a data-cleaning one.

\section{Empirical confirmation}

Row-centring was added to the pipeline and run end-to-end on the real data
(91 respondents after excluding entirely empty submissions, 60 items). Table
\ref{tab:centring-effect} compares the item--item correlation matrix computed
on the raw imputed matrix against the same matrix after row-centring, at the
edge threshold used for the visualised network.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Matrix & Mean $|r|$ & Median $|r|$ & Edges at $|r| \ge 0.30$ \\
\midrule
Raw (uncentred)   & 0.182 & 0.162 & 327 \\
Row-centred       & 0.110 & 0.094 & 42 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Effect of row-centring on the item--item correlation matrix. Centring
removes the acquiescence-bias component, collapsing the edge count at a fixed
threshold by roughly 87\%.}
\label{tab:centring-effect}
\end{table}

The raw network's higher edge count is consistent with the near-complete,
uninformative graph that raw-score similarity is expected to produce: most of
those 327 edges reflect shared agreeableness rather than genuine item-to-item
association. This is the same effect anticipated in the project's frozen
pipeline decisions, now verified against the actual dataset rather than
assumed.

\section{Scope note: what row-centring was \emph{not} used to justify}

No respondent-filtering threshold (e.g., dropping respondents with more than
12 missing items) was introduced as part of this change, in either direction.
Two draft specifications considered such a filter; neither was applied. All
91 respondents retained after removing entirely empty submissions remain in
the analysis, consistent with the repository's existing behaviour prior to
this change.

\section{Repository reorganisation}

The single 788-line \texttt{src/ordinal\_pipeline.py} module was split into
focused, importable modules:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{src/config.py} --- shared constants and \texttt{PipelineConfig}.
  \item \texttt{src/loader.py} --- CSV parsing, missingness identification,
        encoding, and dataset export helpers.
  \item \texttt{src/imputation.py} --- the ordinal logistic regression
        imputer, training, and masking-based validation.
  \item \texttt{src/similarity.py} --- \texttt{row\_centre()} (new) and the
        Spearman correlation computation, plus a raw-vs-centred comparison
        helper.
  \item \texttt{src/metrics.py} --- graph construction and descriptive
        network statistics.
  \item \texttt{src/figures.py} --- network visualisation.
  \item \texttt{src/reporting.py} --- Markdown/JSON summary generation,
        extended to report the raw-vs-centred comparison.
  \item \texttt{src/pipeline.py} --- orchestrates the above; imported by a
        new top-level \texttt{run\_all.py}.
\end{itemize}

The notebook's import was updated to the new module paths, a
\texttt{.gitignore} was added for \texttt{.venv/}, \texttt{\_\_pycache\_\_/},
and notebook checkpoints, and \texttt{run\_all.py} was verified to reproduce
all outputs from a clean invocation.
